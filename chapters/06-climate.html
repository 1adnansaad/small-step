Step 6 · Climate change

A hotter, more erratic climate

The plan runs everything through two futures. Meet them now — BAU and EXT appear on almost every screen that follows.

BAU — Business As Usual

The moderate climate change scenario, assuming sustained global and national efforts to reduce greenhouse gas emissions.

EXT — Extreme

The extreme climate change scenario, assuming limited global and national effort on emissions and fossil-fuel-based economic development.

Temperature

Globally, IPCC's Fifth Assessment Report records warming of 0.85 °C (0.65–1.06) in combined land and ocean surface temperature over 1880–2012. Between the 1850–1900 average and the 2003–2012 average the total increase is 0.78 °C (0.72–0.85). Almost the entire globe warmed over 1901–2012.

Bangladesh has also experienced considerable temperature rise. There is debate about the magnitude of future increases, but consensus that warming will continue here as globally.

1.4–1.9 °C temperature rise across Bangladesh by 2050 under BAU
~2 °C temperature rise by 2050 under EXT

Temperature will rise in all regions of Bangladesh, following the global trend, with further rise expected in the latter half of the century — especially under EXT.

Rainfall

Rainfall is going to be more variable and more erratic. Under BAU there are indications that pre-monsoon and monsoon rainfall will increase, and on an annual basis rainfall is expected to increase in most regions by 2030.

Not evenly distributed By 2050, southern parts of the country along with the eastern hills might instead see reductions in rainfall. Under EXT, higher temperatures bring even more erratic rainfall behaviour along with variation in amounts.

More rain in the north and less in the south, arriving less predictably, is a harder problem than simply "more rain" — it is why so many of the plan's strategies are about storage, drainage and flexibility rather than a single fixed defence.
