Step 21 · Investment

What it costs to build

Implementing BDP 2100 means spending about 2.5% of GDP a year on delta interventions. Bangladesh currently spends 0.8%.

Delta-related spending, share of GDP per year

Present spending0.8%
Required under BDP 21002.5%

Covers new projects plus maintenance of new and old projects.

How the 2.5% splits

  • 0.5% of GDP — funded by the private sector.
  • 2.0% of GDP — executed through the public sector, of which:
    • 0.5% for operations and maintenance;
    • 1.5% under the BDP 2100 Investment Plan.
The O&M problem, stated plainly At present O&M is very much neglected — the actual amount may not even exceed 0.1% of GDP. Not maintaining delta investments has led to rapid deterioration of water infrastructure, and then to complete rebuilding at much higher cost.

The spending path

  1. FY2016About US$ 1.8 billion — pre-BDP 2100 spending levels.
  2. FY2017About US$ 3.5 billion.
  3. FY2031Increasing to US$ 29.6 billion.

Where the money goes first

Priorities have to be set: the list of investment priorities is extensive, and implementation capacity constrains how many large projects can be designed, prepared and delivered at once. Much of the public funding goes to highly capital-intensive work — flood protection, river erosion control, river management, urban and rural water supply and waste management, and urban flood control and drainage.

River management here includes dredging, river training and navigability. Bars are proportional to the largest share.

Not only concrete BDP 2100 projects are not just physical investments. There are major research, knowledge and institutional gaps that require complementary capacity building investment.

The Investment Plan (IP)

80 projects in the first phase, up to 2030 — 65 physical, 15 institutional and knowledge development
BDT 2,978bn total capital investment cost (about US$ 37 billion)

All 80 projects can be started within the next eight years, although given the scale and programmatic nature of some, construction will extend over decades.

How the 80 were chosen

GED asked more than 20 agencies working in the delta to submit their priority projects. That generated 133 candidate projects with total capital costs of BDT 3,753 billion (US$ 47 billion). Candidates were screened, grouped and sequenced using the Adaptive Delta Management methodology, and were included only if they met all three tests:

  1. Expected benefits exceed expected costs.
  2. They contribute to at least one of the six BDP 2100 goals.
  3. They are compatible with the ADM approach.

Of the 133 candidates, 80 met these criteria.
