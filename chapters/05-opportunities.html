Step 5 · Delta opportunities

What the delta gives Bangladesh

BDP 2100 is not only a risk plan. Before it lists the threats, it lists the advantages the delta hands the country — because the strategies exist to protect these.

Land that grows food

The soil and water combination of Bangladesh makes it highly fertile land with multiple cropping opportunities. Combining that natural advantage with seed–fertiliser–irrigation technology intensified cultivation and expanded food production.

Rice production

197312 m tonnes
201836.3 m tonnes

Alongside a very successful population control policy, this brought Bangladesh to food self-sufficiency, with prospects for rice exports.

A testimony to resilience Achieving this despite the multiple risks posed by the delta and climate change says a great deal about Bangladeshi society.

Water that grows fish

Plentiful rivers, freshwater wetlands and lakes give ample scope for fisheries, and marine fishing has now become a major source too. As fisheries grow in importance, the structure of agriculture is slowly changing: the share of the crop sub-sector is falling while fisheries rise, taking value-added and employment shares with them.

Rivers that carry trade

24,000 km of rivers, streams and canals — 7% of the country's surface
Almost all districts connected to Dhaka, Chattogram and Khulna by waterway

Inland waterways are an environmentally friendly, low-cost transport option for passengers and cargo — and especially for the rural poor, for whom inland water transport is also a major source of employment. More focus on river transport can lower transport costs, reduce environmental degradation, conserve budget resources and add jobs.

A sea that opens outward

Open access to the sea is a huge advantage. Beyond serving internal trade, with proper investment Bangladesh can become a regional hub for international sea transportation — the plan points to Rotterdam, Singapore and Hong Kong as examples of what planning and investment can do with such a position.

Energy

Rapidly growing energy demand and the depletion of natural gas have led to a strategy of procuring clean imported coal, LNG and LPG — all bulky cargoes that need port capacity. Alongside Chattogram and Mongla, the ongoing Payra port construction in Patuakhali is part of that response.

Blue economy

Marine fishing is already emerging as a major source of food, exports, income and employment, and there is growing demand for coastal tourism beyond the traditional Cox's Bazar resorts — Kuakata in Patuakhali being one example.

Hold on to this list Everything from here on — the flood strategies, the freshwater strategies, the investment plan — exists to keep these advantages available in 2100.
