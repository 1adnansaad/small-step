Step 19 · Cross-cutting issues, part 2

Transport, sea, energy and earthquakes

The last four cross-cutting themes — three about seizing an opportunity, one about a risk that has nothing to do with water at all.

Cross-cutting 4

Dynamizing the Inland Water Transport System

Bangladesh is crisscrossed by about 24,000 km of rivers, streams and canals — 7% of the country's surface — and has always enjoyed the natural advantage of inland navigation.

  • Regular dredging to maintain flow and transport in the rivers.
  • Develop reliable water system conditions for long term sustainable IWT through capital and maintenance dredging of the Padma, Meghna, Jamuna, Brahmaputra, Dharla, Arial Khan, Kushiyara, Gorai and Manu.
  • Regular dredging for Ghashiakhali and other channels in the Sundarbans.
  • Ensure efficient and equitable use of sand through regular shifting of the 'Balu-mahal' (sand quarry), with specific guidelines for managing soil and sediment from dredging.
  • BIWTA to cooperate and coordinate with BWDB to provide optimal levels of surface water for navigation.
  • Develop the navigation network according to societal and economic demand.
  • Develop, maintain and operate inland river ports, landing ferry ghats and terminal facilities.
  • Contribute to transboundary water aspects by developing mutual understanding and cooperation.
  • Development of riverine and maritime ports.
  • Initiatives for activating transboundary waterways.
Cross-cutting 5

Advancing the Blue Economy

With maritime border disputes settled with Myanmar and India in 2014, the blue economy is now considered a new 'development space'. Shipping, coastal shipping, sea ports, ship building and recycling, marine fisheries, sea salt, coastal tourism, ocean energy, land reclamation, maritime surveillance, human resources development and governance are the identified priority sectors. Sixteen different approaches have been proposed, with regional partnerships ranked high on the agenda, and MoUs signed with India.

  • Quick completion of a multidimensional survey of marine resources.
  • Increase the number of sea-going vessels; modernise and build capacity at the sea ports.
  • Increase both shallow and deep sea fishing.
  • Introduction of eco-tourism and private sector initiatives in sea cruise.
  • Keeping the coasts and sea ports pollution free.
Cross-cutting 6

Renewable Energy

Bangladesh is expected to have enormous potential in solar, wind, biomass and hydropower.

  • Develop a long term renewable energy policy and strategy, and a master plan for at least 50 years, involving public and private investment.
  • Promote research on renewable energy technology in universities and research institutions, and build capacity for its application.
  • Enhance green growth through research and development of renewable technologies, including the clean development mechanism (CDM).
  • Devise innovative financing packages — grant funding and low interest financing — to address affordability for both grid and off-grid renewable projects.
The headline energy target At least 30% of energy production from renewable sources by 2041, in the context of becoming a prosperous country.
Cross-cutting 7

Earthquakes

Bangladesh and the north-eastern states of India have long been among the seismically active regions of the world, with numerous large earthquakes over the past 200 years.

  • Strengthen earthquake management and enhance the capacity to cope with earthquakes.
  • Design earthquake-proof structures — barrages, regulators, sluices, embankments, cross-dams, roads, bridges and buildings — in conformity with the Bangladesh National Building Code or other approved standards.
  • Formulate a proper land use plan for building construction in municipal areas.
  • Conduct a detailed study to identify faults and epicentres.
That is the whole strategy set Two national strategies, six hotspot strategy sets, seven cross-cutting themes. The remaining screens are about turning them into money, projects and institutions.
