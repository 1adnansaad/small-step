Step 20 · Macroeconomic scenarios

With the plan, and without it

The macroeconomic framework of BDP 2100 is the first attempt to quantify the economic impact of climate change in Bangladesh by linking economic variables directly to climate parameters. It exists to answer one question: what happens if the Delta Plan is not adopted?

How the model is built

Three endogenous variables — economic growth, employment and poverty. Two exogenous variables — natural hazards and climate change. Plus policy variables covering population and labour force, public and private investment, sectoral investment including what is needed to offset climate risks, macroeconomic policy, transboundary water dialogue, and institutional reform and good governance.

The two policy options

Option 1 — Business As Usual (BAU)

Essentially the government's Vision 2021, Perspective Plan and Seventh Five Year Plan — with the adverse effects of climate change and natural hazards written in. Every policy is in place except the Delta Plan. This is today's environment, where no coordinated effort to manage delta risks exists.

Option 2 — Delta Plan (DP)

BAU plus adoption of BDP 2100: strong climate change and delta-related adaptation measures, aimed at higher and sustainable growth in the face of weather-related hazards.

What happens under BAU

The results are striking. GDP growth starts falling as the adverse effects gain momentum, and the damage compounds through the whole economy:

  • The efficiency of capital falls.
  • Outmigration from vulnerable districts to cities increases, adding to urbanisation pressure.
  • Agricultural productivity falls.
  • Land degradation reduces land availability and raises land prices.
  • Urban land availability falls relative to demand as migration adds pressure.
  • Urban production costs rise as flooding and drainage damage properties, enterprises and infrastructure.
  • Urban water shortages, water quality and sanitation risks add health costs and reduce quality of life.

Under BAU, Bangladesh is unable to reach upper middle income status or eliminate extreme poverty by 2030.

What changes under the Delta Plan

With BDP 2100 adopted, the negative effects of climate change and natural hazards are considerably reduced. The economy avoids a disruption of the growth effort and secures an average GDP growth rate of about 9%. On the back of that — and by avoiding the loss of agricultural income and rural livelihoods — Bangladesh secures both objectives: eliminating extreme poverty and reaching upper middle income status by 2030.
