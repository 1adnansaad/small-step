Step 9 · Sea level, salinity, cyclones

Rising sea, creeping salt

Sea level rise and the salinity intrusion that follows it are, in the plan's words, the most prominent issues now facing the Bangladesh delta.

0.2 – 1.0 m sea level rise predicted for the Bay of Bengal by 2100, from low to high emission scenarios (IPCC, 2013)

Salt moving inland

Rising sea level impedes fresh water availability along the coast and pushes the salinity front further in. Both surface water and soil salinity may increase.

Cyclones and storm surges

Low-lying coastal areas are highly vulnerable to cyclones. Nearly every year cyclones hit the coastal region, and a severe cyclone strikes the country every three years on average.

Rising sea surface temperature and sea level are likely to increase the intensity of cyclonic storm surges and the depth and extent of the coastal inundation they cause. The IPCC reports that future storm surges and related coastal floods in Bangladesh will likely become more severe as tropical cyclones intensify.
