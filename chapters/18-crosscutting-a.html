Step 18 · Cross-cutting issues, part 1

Land, food and shared rivers

Some problems belong to no single hotspot. The plan handles seven of them separately — here are the first three.

Cross-cutting 1

Sustainable Land Use and Spatial Planning

Land strategy covers natural areas for agriculture, forests and watercourses as well as urban and industrial needs — which makes spatial planning an integral part of land use management.

  • Develop effective policy guidelines and rules for the Balu Mahal and sediment management.
  • Preserve and conserve agricultural land from floods or erosion to sustain food grain production.
  • Prevention of salinity intrusion and desertification.
  • Management of newly accreted land in the Meghna estuary.
  • Sustainable coastal land management for enhancing agricultural and non-agricultural land.
  • Development of a Digital Land Resource Management System.
  • Reviewing, updating and enacting laws on Alluvion and Diluvion to improve land administration of accreting and reclaimed land.
  • Formulation of necessary laws and acts to establish land zoning.
  • Increase climate change adaptation capacity for land management.
  • Spatial land use planning for urbanisation, and optimisation of land use.
  • Formulation of a Spatial Planning and Land Resource Management Act.
  • Enhance afforestation and plantation in the coastal zone for stabilising land.
  • Restoration and protection of soil health; protection against erosion and land loss.
  • Integrated management of coastal water infrastructure to protect land.
Cross-cutting 2

Agriculture, Food Security, Nutrition and Livelihoods

Agriculture is likely to bear the main brunt of output losses from climate change and natural disasters. By 2100, climate change could impose costs on the Bangladesh economy significantly higher than the estimated global average loss.

  • Increasing resilience of agricultural production systems.
  • Diversification in agricultural output and livelihoods.
  • Lower greenhouse gas emissions from agricultural land.
  • Encourage establishing commercial farms.
  • Introduction of aquaponics to culture fish and plants together.
  • Using nanotechnology in agriculture for processing, distribution and packaging.
  • Introduce a precision agriculture model.
  • Encouraging solar power in irrigation.
  • Improved farm practices and technologies to mediate negative climate change impacts.
  • Preservation of ecosystems for plants, wild animals, fish and birds; encourage fruit tree plantation.
  • Improve wetland management in haor areas for fisheries development.
  • Maintaining biodiversity to ensure long term fish availability.
  • Sustainable marine fisheries resources management.
  • Production of climate resilient livestock.
Cross-cutting 3

Transboundary Water Management

Sitting in the central position of the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna basins and receiving substantial transboundary flows, Bangladesh needs cooperation with India, Nepal, Bhutan, Myanmar and China to develop further — on the general understanding that joint cooperation outweighs individual development.

  • Development of an action plan that takes upstream countries' water usage into consideration.
  • Selection of prospective embankment sites considering upstream flow, with understanding and cooperation from upstream countries.
  • Multi-track water diplomacy to prevent or peacefully resolve conflicts.
  • Continuing efforts to sign a treaty on sharing the waters of the Teesta and all other transboundary rivers.
  • Initiate demand-based common river basin management schemes.
  • Third party involvement — a multilateral or bilateral development partner or country — to resolve transboundary water issues.
  • Improved basin-wide flood forecasting.
