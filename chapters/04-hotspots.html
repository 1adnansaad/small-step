Step 4 · Hotspots

The six hotspots

Every strategy, project and rupee in BDP 2100 is eventually attached to one of these six areas. Learn them once and the rest of the plan reads easily.

Coastal Zone

27,738 sq. km

Cyclones, storm surges, tidal flooding and salinity intrusion. Home to the polders, the Sundarbans and the country's exposure to sea level rise.

Barind & Drought Prone Areas

22,848 sq. km

The dry north-west. Agricultural drought, falling groundwater tables and reduced dry-season surface water make this the freshwater-availability hotspot.

Haor & Flash Flood Areas

16,574 sq. km

The north-eastern wetland basins. Long annual flooding allows only a single rice crop, making this one of the country's food-insecure regions.

Chattogram Hill Tracts

13,295 sq. km

Hills and their related coastal plains. Landslides upstream connect directly to sedimentation and recurrent dredging downstream.

River System & Estuaries

35,204 sq. km

The largest hotspot and the backbone of the delta. Bank erosion, sedimentation, navigability and estuary management all live here.

Urban Areas

19,823 sq. km

Drainage, waterlogging, water supply, sanitation and river pollution — in a country where the majority will live in cities by 2045.
