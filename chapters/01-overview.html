Step 1 · Overview

The largest delta in the world

Bangladesh sits on the biggest active delta on earth. That single fact explains both its remarkable food production and nearly every risk the Delta Plan is written to manage.

Development has gained tremendous momentum over the last decade, and the country now aspires to reach upper middle income status by 2030. Effective policies, an abundant labour supply, very fertile land, plentiful water and monsoon rains laid the foundation for that record.

But the same delta that made those gains possible also creates the country's hardest development problems.

160m people, in a country of 1,47,570 km² including its rivers
~1,200 people per square kilometre — among the densest on earth
6th most vulnerable country in the world to natural hazard risk
70% of the land lies less than one metre above sea level
Regular features, not rare disasters Tidal surge, salinity, flooding, river erosion and cyclones are ordinary parts of the year in Bangladesh — a consequence of the deltaic formation of the country, the configuration of its rivers, and climate change.

Why a 100-year horizon?

The opportunities and the risks arising where water, climate change and the environment meet are all long term in nature. A road or a power plant can be planned over five years. A delta cannot: embankments, river training, land reclamation and salinity control play out over decades, and the climate they must withstand in 2080 is decided by choices made now.

Learning from Dutch delta management, the Government took technical assistance from the Netherlands and set out to plan on the timescale the delta actually works on — to 2100.
