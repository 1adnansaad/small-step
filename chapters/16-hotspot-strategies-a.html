Step 16 · Hotspot strategies, part 1

Coast, Barind and haor

Three of the six hotspots, each with a different core problem: salt, drought and a single annual crop.

Hotspot 1

Coastal Zone

The Coastal Zone will remain hazardous for coastal floods for the foreseeable future. Cyclones and storm surges will keep developing in the Bay of Bengal, while high river discharges and monsoon precipitation put enormous pressure on the drainage capacity of the coastal delta. On top of possible changes in discharge regimes and sea level, economic development and demographic change add their own pressure.

  • Combating storm surge and salinity intrusion through effective management of existing polders.
  • Increase drainage capacity and reduce flood risks.
  • Balancing water supply and demand for sustainable growth.
  • Reclaim new land in the Coastal Zone.
  • Sundarbans conservation.
  • Restoration of dead or low-flowing rivers, and basin-wide management of cross-boundary rivers to increase the supply of fresh water.
Tidal River Management (TRM) TRM is an effective strategy for reducing waterlogging, increasing river navigability and reclaiming land. BDP 2100 recommends both gaining efficiency in TRM and expanding it to address these coastal problems.
Hotspot 2

Barind and Drought Prone Areas

This hotspot was selected as the representative case for drought — that is, for freshwater availability — in the country. The strategies support the delta goals here while building on development activities already under way.

  • Balancing supply and demand for sustainable and inclusive growth.
  • Management of cross-boundary water issues, including river basin developments.
  • Minimising losses due to floods and drainage congestion.
  • Ensuring water supply and sanitation.
  • Encouraging excavation of ponds and digging of wells to retain rainwater.
Hotspot 3

Haor and Flash Flood Areas

The haor basin is one of the food insecure regions of the country, with both opportunities and constraints for its inhabitants. Because annual flooding lasts so long, only a single crop — rice — can be grown, and food insecurity follows from that.

  • Protect agriculture and vulnerable communities from floods.
  • Achieving fresh water security.
  • Management of river and water resources.
  • Sustainable management of the haor ecosystem and its biodiversity.
  • Integrated land and water resources management.
