Step 13 · Adaptive Delta Management

Planning for a future you cannot predict

This is the intellectual heart of BDP 2100, and the thing that makes it different from the plans that came before.

The problem is easy to state and hard to solve: how should policies, programmes and investments be formulated in the short to medium term so that they stay consistent with long term outcomes that are inherently uncertain?

Water, climate change and the environment are heavily influenced by the behaviour of nature, which is often not predictable. Yet there are immediate challenges that must be addressed now, and the choices made now will shape what is possible in 2100.

The answer: be adaptive, on a cycle

  1. Set the destination Develop a broad, long term vision and mission for the Bangladesh Delta at the end of the 21st century.
  2. Work backwards Define short and medium term goals as steps toward that vision, rather than fixed end points.
  3. Use the best available information Develop strategies and policy options under different assumptions about external outcomes — not a single forecast.
  4. Every 5 years Update the scenarios and strategies as new information becomes available, on the Five Year Plan cycle.
Goals as moving targets The goals, strategies, policies, institutions and investments are deliberately treated as moving targets — adaptive to changing natural events, so the country can respond appropriately and still stay on course for the long term vision.

'No regret' measures

The strategies are chosen to be flexible in their measures, actions, timeframe and treatment of uncertainty. The plan calls them 'no regret' measures: effective and beneficial across a range of possible futures, rather than optimal for one and wasteful in the others.

Two failure modes it avoids

  • Under-building: investing in measures that are rapidly outdated because conditions changed.
  • Over-building: over-dimensioning structures that later prove unnecessary, wasting scarce resources.

Delta scenarios — covering climate change, sea level rise and socio-economic development together — are the tool used to test how robust each strategy and measure is before it is committed to.

A useful rule of thumb from the plan Up to 2030, rapidly changing socio-economic conditions matter more than climate change. Beyond 2030, and depending on which scenario unfolds, climate change may have a notable impact.

This adaptive approach — including how investment projects get selected — is what links short and medium term development targets to the long term goal of sustained, climate-sensitive development.
