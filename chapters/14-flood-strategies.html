Step 14 · National level · Flood Risk (FR)

Flood risk strategies

Flooding is the one challenge that is nation-wide rather than hotspot-specific, so the plan tackles it first and at national level.

Three principles behind every FR strategy

  • Strategies conducive to economic development without degrading the environment.
  • Developing a climate change resilient Bangladesh through optimal use of natural resources.
  • Climate resilient development through a participatory process.
Strategy FR 1

Protecting Economic Strongholds and Critical Infrastructure

Areas essential to economic growth need a high flood protection standard — that is what attracts the investment that lets the economy grow. Typical measures are embankments, barriers, erosion control and efficient drainage; most are already in place at a basic level. Flood-proof building is also needed for hospitals, power stations, industrial plants and the major communication networks between them.

  • FR 1.1 — Protection by development and improvement of embankments, barriers and water control structures (including ring dikes) for economic priority zones and major urban centres.
  • FR 1.2 — Construct adaptive and flood-storm-surge resilient buildings.
  • FR 1.3 — Adopt spatial planning and flood hazard zoning based on flood intensity.
  • FR 1.4 — Improve Flood Early Warning System services, both basin-wise and hotspot-wise.
  • FR 1.5 — Improvement of drainage.
Do no harm downstream Flood control measures must ensure projects do not create inundation problems elsewhere — by creating room for rivers so they can follow their natural courses and find their pathways to the sea.
Strategy FR 2

Equipping the Flood Management and Drainage (FMD) Schemes for the Future

The country has invested heavily in flood control and drainage development over the past 60 years. Those schemes are now in urgent need of maintenance and, in selected areas, remodelling. Future designs must account for projected climate and hydrological change, sea level rise, and changed land use, infrastructure and urbanisation — while staying flexible enough to remain efficient across a range of conditions.

  • FR 2.1 — Management of 'submerged char' before any flood management or river flow management initiative.
  • FR 2.2 — Drainage improvement inside FMDI schemes.
  • FR 2.3 — Restoration of water bodies and connectivity among and between FMDI schemes.
  • FR 2.4 — Restoration, redesign and modification of embankments and structures where necessary.
  • FR 2.5 — River management, excavation and smart dredging, preceded by appropriate feasibility study.
Strategy FR 3

Safeguarding Livelihoods of Vulnerable Communities

Alongside protecting economic strongholds there is an obligation to vulnerable regions and communities — the principle of 'leaving no one behind'. This is not about creating a flood-free Bangladesh for all; at the current level of economic development that is not feasible within the coming decades, and given the difficulty of maintaining flood infrastructure in remote and poor areas it is not even desirable in the medium term. It means two things instead: mitigating the most undesired effects of large and extreme floods, and enabling those affected to recover quickly afterwards.

  • FR 3.1 — Management of rivers and embankments with provision for the fastest drainage of water during monsoon and flood.
  • FR 3.2 — Improved river management and better operation and maintenance of FMD schemes.
  • FR 3.3 — Extension and improvement of multipurpose cyclone shelters and their services, along with emergency services.
  • FR 3.4 — Development of flood proof water supply and improved drainage systems.
  • FR 3.5 — Flood and storm surge proofing of housing and other critical infrastructure, supported by quick emergency services.
  • FR 3.6 — Social safety net and enhancement of rapid response recovery packages.
  • FR 3.7 — River management plus improved flood management, drainage, O&M and flow management.
  • FR 3.8 — Protection of chars and their population, along with alternative livelihood arrangements.
