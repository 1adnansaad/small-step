Step 7 · Too much water, too little water

Floods and droughts

The same country floods for part of the year and runs dry for another part of it. Both are managed by the same plan.

Floods

The Ganges, the Brahmaputra and the Meghna meet in Bangladesh to form the largest delta in the world. As a result most of the country is floodplain and delta.

70% of the total area is less than one metre above sea level
10% of the land area is made up of lakes and rivers
20–25% of the country inundated in an average year, from river spilling and drainage congestion

Heavy monsoon rains — especially over the highlands — combined with frequent tropical storms in the coastal region trigger flooding again and again. The extreme situation arises when the three major rivers peak at the same time.

Under EXT, in the Barind The Barind region adjacent to the right bank of the Jamuna is projected to be more inundated in future.

Droughts

The droughts that occur in Bangladesh are not meteorological droughts but agricultural droughts — which could also be called severe moisture stress.

32–48 dry days in drought-prone agro-ecological regions, from 24 March to 21 May
5–15 days above 40 °C within those same regions in that period

Some soils there also have low moisture-holding capacity, producing different degrees of droughtiness across the region.

The Barind: a success that shifted the problem

The government's Barind Region Irrigation Project changed the profile of the Barind, rapidly transforming the area into an agrarian green field with diversified agriculture based on rice, fruits and vegetables — and contributing substantially to lowering poverty in the North-West.

But the risk moved forward, not away Surface water is reduced by upstream diversion of river water in India, and inadequate dry-season rainfall continues to lower the water table. Climate change that reduces dry-season rainfall further could hurt agriculture in the Barind Tract.

That pairing — a real gain, followed by a new dependency — is exactly why BDP 2100 insists on planning for the long term rather than project by project.
