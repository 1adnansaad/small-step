Step 17 · Hotspot strategies, part 2

Hills, rivers and cities

The remaining three hotspots — including the largest one, and the one where most Bangladeshis will soon live.

Hotspot 4

Chattogram Hill Tracts

Both the hills and their related coastal plains are considered together, because problems in the hills — landslides — cause problems on the plains — sedimentation and recurrent dredging. Unusually for Bangladesh, most of the catchment of the important rivers in the Eastern Hill area lies inside the country's borders, which offers excellent conditions for integrated river basin management and the chance to address problems holistically.

  • Protect economic zones and towns from floods and storm surges.
  • Ensure water security and sustainable sanitation.
  • Ensure integrated river management.
  • Maintain ecological balance and values (assets).
  • Develop a multi-purpose resources management system for sustainable growth.
Hotspot 5

River Systems and Estuaries

The major rivers are the backbone of the delta system and play a central role in BDP 2100, so a long term strategy for them has national importance. Many of Bangladesh's water-related challenges trace back to these dynamic rivers.

  • Provide adequate room for the river and infrastructure to reduce flood risk.
  • Improvement of conveyance capacity, and stabilisation of the rivers.
  • Provide fresh water of sufficient quantity and quality.
  • Maintain the ecological balance and values (assets) of the rivers.
  • Allow safe and reliable waterway transport in the river system.
  • Develop a sediment management strategy including a strong capital dredging and maintenance programme.
  • Strengthen river and estuary management in newly accreted lands.
  • Arrange capital and maintenance dredging in important rivers — the Padma, Meghna, Jamuna, Brahmaputra, Dharala, Arial Khan, Kushiyara, Gorai, Monu and others.
  • Appropriate and effective salinity management for southern-zone rivers during the dry season.
  • Formulate policy and guidelines for proper management of 'Balu Mohal' and dredged materials or soils.
Hotspot 6

Urban Areas

Urban centres will keep growing under rural–urban migration, and by 2045 the majority of the population will live in cities. The focus here is water, but general urban planning problems — uncontrolled growth and haphazard development — are tightly connected to water issues like flood risk management. High population density and a range of socio-economic and political factors underlie the currently challenged state of urban service delivery.

  • Increase drainage capacity and reduce flood risk and waterlogging in urban areas.
  • Enhance water security and water use efficiency in urban areas.
  • Regulate and monitor pollution of rivers and other water bodies from industry and human sources.
  • Conserve and preserve urban wetlands and ecosystems and promote their wise use.
  • Develop effective urban institutions and governance.
  • Integrated and sustainable use of urban land and water resources.
  • Improved urban services — water supply, sanitation, wastewater and solid waste management — with special emphasis on the disposal of medical, electronic and other hazardous waste.
  • Control and monitoring of water pollution caused by industry and other sources.
