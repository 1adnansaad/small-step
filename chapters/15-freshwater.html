Step 15 · National level · Fresh Water (FW)

Fresh water strategies

Demand for water is rising from every direction at once. Two national strategies cover having enough of it, and it being clean enough to use.

The constraint that shapes everything 93% of the catchment of the Ganges–Brahmaputra–Meghna lies outside Bangladesh's borders. Most of the country's water is decided elsewhere before it arrives.

Demand is intensified by high demographic growth, rapid and unplanned urbanisation, high sectoral demand from agriculture, fisheries, transportation and industry, and climate change — while the rich but vulnerable ecosystem needs water too, and availability swings between wet and dry seasons.

Strategy FW 1

Ensure Water Availability by Balancing Supply and Demand for Sustainable and Inclusive Growth

  • FW 1.1 — Ensure optimum water resource management in the country following basin-wide management, along with construction of necessary embankments.
  • FW 1.2 — New irrigation schemes for the major rivers of the country.
  • FW 1.3 — Excavation of local water reservoirs (canals, ponds and baors) for restoration of water and rainwater harvesting.
  • FW 1.4 — Construction of rubber dams, preceded by appropriate feasibility study.
  • FW 1.5 — Enhancement of freshwater flows in urban and regional rivers.
  • FW 1.6 — Restoration of natural reservoirs and water bodies along with their biodiversity conservation.
  • FW 1.7 — Preserving the ground water level by restricting excessive extraction of ground water.
  • FW 1.8 — Increase fresh water flow in urban and rural rivers and control river pollution.

The Padma Barrage

Construction of the Padma Barrage, with proper feasibility work, is proposed as a major activity under this strategy. It would make optimum use of Padma water during the dry season possible — ensuring irrigation water for the Coastal Zone and the Barind and drought prone areas, while increasing freshwater flow in the rivers of those areas. Agricultural and fisheries production would rise substantially and salinity would decrease.

The low-tech half of the strategy Alongside the large structures, the plan puts heavy emphasis on conserving natural water bodies, excavating and re-excavating ponds and canals, rainwater harvesting, and re-establishing the connectivity of canals with rivers — as effective ways of increasing dry-season fresh water.
Strategy FW 2

Maintaining Water Quality for Health, Livelihoods and Ecosystems

Water quality is a growing concern, with many rivers and wetlands at serious environmental risk. To ensure healthy lives, livelihoods and ecosystems, water quality needs to be maintained according to the relevant rules and regulations.

  • FW 2.1 — Appropriate waste management and reduction of pollution in urban and rural areas.
  • FW 2.2 — Monitoring and control of pollution.
  • FW 2.3 — Action research for improved ecosystem services.
